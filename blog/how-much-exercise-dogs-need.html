How Much Exercise Does Your Dog Really Need?

✍️ Marcus Reed📅 September 8, 2026⏱️ 6 min read

A tired dog is a happy dog — up to a point. Too little exercise causes boredom and destruction; too much can cause injury. Here is how to find your dog's sweet spot.

General Guidelines by Energy Level

Dog TypeDaily ExerciseExamples
Low energy30–45 minutesBulldogs, Basset Hounds, senior dogs
Moderate energy1–1.5 hoursBeagles, Labs, Poodles
High energy2+ hours, often splitBorder Collies, Huskies, Malinois

Age Changes Everything

  • Puppies: short, frequent play; avoid repetitive high-impact running on hard surfaces while growth plates are open.
  • Adults: the figures above, adjusted to breed and fitness.
  • Seniors: shorter, gentler walks plus mental enrichment to keep the mind active.

Exercise Is More Than Walking

Mental work tires a dog faster than many owners realise. Ten minutes of sniffing, puzzle toys, or obedience games can be as valuable as a longer march around the block.

Signs of Too Little Exercise

  • Destructive chewing or digging.
  • Excessive barking, pacing or whining.
  • Weight gain and low mood.

Signs of Too Much Exercise

  • Limping, stiffness, or reluctance to move after walks.
  • Exhaustion that lasts into the next day.
  • Paw pad wear or overheating — especially in hot weather.
Match the exercise to the individual dog, not the stereotype. A "lazy" breed can need more than you think, and a "busy" breed can need less when they're older or unwell.

Beat the Heat and Cold

Walk in the early morning or evening during hot months, and protect paws from hot pavement. In winter, shorten walks for short-coated and senior dogs, and consider a coat.

This article is for general information only and is not a substitute for veterinary advice.
