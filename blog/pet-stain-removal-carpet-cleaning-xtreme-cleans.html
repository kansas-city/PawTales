Why Pet Stains Are Harder Than Regular Spills

Pet accidents are not like spilled juice. Urine is a cocktail of urea, uric acid, hormones and pheromones. As it dries, the liquid wicks through the carpet fibres, through the backing, and deep into the padding beneath. Uric acid forms crystals that bond to the fibres and are insoluble in plain water.

That is why a surface spray or a rental machine rarely fixes the problem. You clean the top, the smell returns on a humid day, and your pet returns to the same spot because the pheromone marker is still there. Beating pet odour means reaching at the source — and that requires serious extraction power.

The Limits of DIY Cleaning

  • Over-wetting: supermarkets and rental units often leave carpet too wet, which can grow mould in the padding.
  • Wrong chemistry: many household detergents leave sticky residue that actually attracts new dirt.
  • Hidden odour: without deep extraction, the odour source stays in the pad and keeps releasing smell.
  • Pet re-marking: if the scent marker remains, the behaviour usually continues.

None of this means DIY is useless — but for genuine pet-stain and odour recovery, professional extraction is the reliable path.
